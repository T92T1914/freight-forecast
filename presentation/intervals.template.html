A separate retrospective experiment

A range still has to earn its coverage.

I added residual intervals around the saved BTS forecasts to ask how coverage changes when earlier errors stop describing the present. The intervals use the existing point predictions. They do not retrain the model or improve its earlier point forecast result.

The fixed Ridge interval covered 59 of 72 months, below the nominal 90 percent target. Rolling and adaptive Ridge intervals each covered 66 months, with greater mean widths. Last observation had lower mean interval score than Ridge under all three methods. Coverage alone can reward an unnecessarily wide range, so width and score stay visible together.

Updating the residual window did not improve every metric. Around last observation, rolling and adaptive intervals covered more months than the fixed interval, but their mean interval scores were worse: 9.353 and 9.303 versus 9.117. The seasonal baseline had high coverage with much wider ranges. These tradeoffs are retained without choosing a winner.

These are BTS Freight Transportation Services Index points, with 2000 averaging 100. They are not shipment counts. All 648 recorded intervals were finite, with no missing interval. The 2020 to 2025 outcomes had already been inspected. This is not an untouched confirmation test or an operational calibration guarantee.

All nine conditions

Each condition uses the same 72 evaluation months. Coverage is the fraction of actual values inside the issued range. The nominal 90 percent Winkler score adds width and a penalty of 20 times the distance outside the interval. Lower score is better. The score always uses nominal alpha 0.1, even when the adaptive internal level changes.

Calendar years expose the misses

The full period average hides short periods of poor coverage. The fixed Ridge interval covered only 7 of 12 months in 2021. Every year remains below, including misses and wider intervals. These dependent monthly observations do not support a general method ranking.

What was known before each interval

The initial calibration uses 60 monthly absolute errors from 2015 to 2019. Fixed intervals keep that history. Rolling and adaptive intervals use the most recent 60 observed errors. Each range is issued before adding its target's error. Adaptive alpha changes only after the issued interval is scored, using the declared step of 0.005. No outcome was used to select a new window or step.

The point policy had already been selected using development data that include the calibration period. The source is a later revised historical snapshot. Publication delays and historical data vintages are not replayed. An observation step is therefore not a claim about what could have been published on that calendar date.
